Tribeca Emerald

Handwoven Emerald Matka Silk · Changeant Weave

The Tribeca Skirt is a high waisted full length skirt tailored with dropped, deep box pleats and hidden pockets, making an elegant and very comfortable skirt.

The fabric for this skirt is exquisite. Tailored with handspun matka silk, handwoven in jewel-tone emerald green with a black silk weft to create complex tonal layers using a technique made popular in 18th century ball gowns (changeant silk). This full skirt has a striking, dark iridescence. It’s a real showstopper.

Details

  • Full skirt
  • Handwoven and handspun matka silk
  • Jewel-tone emerald green with black silk weft (changeant weave)
  • High waist and dropped, deep box pleats
  • Hidden pockets
  • Full length
  • Hidden left side zipper and top button loop closure

HARTWICK · HERITAGE CRAFT ARCHIVE

Made By Masters

Textile History dates back to the beginning of human civilization. Hartwick specializes in hand spun and handwoven fabrics, natural and ayurvedic dyes, and exquisite heritage handwork relying on rare knowledge and the natural cycles of the sun, wind and rain.

01 · KHADI LIMITED BY NATURE

Wearing handspun and handwoven fabric has a different feeling than wearing industrially produced fabrics; handwoven natural fibres drape and breathe with a supple softness in a rhythm that can only be created by skilled human hands. From the harvest to the yarn to the weave, the material is made by hand without the use of machines, and without electricity.

Hand spinning is an art form where natural fibers, such as cotton, wool or silk, are drawn out and twisted together by hand to form a yarn. Typically spun using a charkha (spinning wheel), it requires great skill to pull the fiber in a continuous rhythm into a strong, fine thread without breaking.

Handloom weaving is a labour of love, patience, and rhythm. Threads are stretched across a wooden loom and interlaced by hand and foot pedals without electricity, creating a breathable, living textile with a subtle, human irregularity.

Derived from locally sourced plants and minerals, natural dyes draw from thousands of years of Ayurvedic knowledge. Master Colorists use wild indigo, pomegranate bark and seeds, tamarind, turmeric, marigold, jaggery, ash, and iron—harmonizing with the sun, wind, and mineral-rich water.

The art of block printing by hand has been passed down from generation to generation. Master artisans carve intricate motifs into seasoned teakwood blocks and stamp each colour layer by hand with remarkable precision.
